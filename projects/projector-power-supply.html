Cordless projector power supply

A battery-powered supply, built from a saw's electronics, that runs our event projector for 2.5 to 3 hours with no outlet.

Hardware3D printingCAD

At a glance

Problem
The projector needed power at venues without a convenient outlet.
Built with
The battery management board from a Craftsman V20 saw, a 3D-printed case, and a 6Ah V20 battery
Result
About 2.5 to 3 hours of runtime, enough for most of our gigs

The idea

I thought I had broken my Craftsman V20 circular saw, because the motor would not spin. It turned out the motor had just worked itself out of the bearing and could have been fixed, but by then I had bought other tools. The saw's battery management board was still perfectly good, so I repurposed it to deliver a steady 18 to 20 volts to our projector.

What I built

I modeled a case in CAD and 3D-printed it to hold the board. I swapped out the tool's switch for rails that accept a Craftsman V20 battery, and ran a power line from the board to the projector.

How it performs

A 6Ah battery runs the projector for roughly 2.5 to 3 hours, which works for most of our gigs. Paired with a wireless HDMI dongle, the projector needs no cables at all.

Where it came from

It came out of running TriviaNight at venues.
