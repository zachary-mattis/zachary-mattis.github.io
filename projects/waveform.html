WaveForm

An app I built for Miller Electric and Pool Supply in Somerset, PA that prints the store's labels and signs, keeps its catalog in step with the point-of-sale system, and runs its charge accounts.

SoftwarePythonFlaskReportLab

At a glance

For
Miller Electric and Pool Supply, Somerset, PA
The need
Consistent price labels, shelf signs and scan labels; a way to keep the point-of-sale system in step with the catalog; and charge accounts handled without handwritten slips.
Built with
Python, Flask, ReportLab, pypdfium2 and waitress
Scale
About 42,000 lines of Python, including an automated test suite

Where it started

I first helped the store with print materials like mailers, labels and business cards. WaveForm is where that work turned into a full app: one place to keep the catalog and produce everything the store prints.

What it does

  • Catalog. Add items by typing, pasting from a spreadsheet or uploading a CSV. Every cell can be edited before saving, and a UPC that looks like it lost its leading 0 gets a warning with a one-click fix.
  • Labels and signs. Prints product labels (Avery 8160), scan labels with UPC-A barcodes (Avery 8195), placards (Avery 5371 or 28371) and shelf-edge strips, plus store signs from quarter-page to legal size, multi-price signs and free-text notices with a live preview.
  • Reprint tracking. The app remembers what each label and sign showed when it was last printed. When a price, name or barcode changes, the item appears in a "needs reprinting" list.
  • Point-of-sale sync. Exports the changed and new products as the upload files the store's point-of-sale system expects. A read-only health report finds duplicate barcodes, missing barcodes and names the system will mangle.
  • Charge accounts. Customer records, charge tickets with scanning and catalog prices, customer discounts, numbered monthly invoices, statements with aging, payments, credit memos for returns, and PDF emails to customers.
  • Safety nets. Daily backups kept for 30 days with an optional second copy off the computer, a backup before every risky change, and undo for bulk edits.

How it is built

The interface is Flask. ReportLab and pypdfium2 generate the PDFs, waitress serves the app, and an automated test suite covers it. It runs on the store's Mac.

A note on privacy

The repository is private because the app holds a real business's data: its catalog, customers and invoices. I am happy to walk through it in an interview.
